The drums are four separate outputs, not one "drums" bus — Kick, Snare, Hats and Perc each get their own volume, delay and reverb, so a dub mix can pull one drum into the echo while the rest stays dry. Bass, Chords, Lead and Pads play on a real scale — natural minor, dorian, mixolydian or minor pentatonic — across two rows of keys, not a chromatic piano squeezed onto glass. The Sampler records whatever the mic hears and drops it into the same grid.

One drop, not a sprint.

68
BPM · DEFAULT

TowerDub opens at 68 — not a race for a bigger number, but a way of feeling the groove: more room between hits, space for a phrase to stretch out, and a nod to where reggae's own foundation rhythms actually sit. Felt in half-time, it's never sluggish — it lands close to a 130–140 tempo, just with more air in it. The clock itself runs on lookahead scheduling against the audio hardware, never a UI timer, so the one-drop stays put even when the screen is busy doing something else.

Every knob goes somewhere.

No dead zones, no potard that only nudges an EQ half a decibel. A handful of controls carry a real range, from what you already know to somewhere new.

BassMorph
Sub-octave hum on one end, pure sine in the middle where it's always lived, a filtered saw trace on the other — never leaves roots territory, just widens it.
ChordsMorph
Low end opens onto a small drawbar organ; the skank stab you already know is still sitting at the middle of the dial.
LeadMorph
Past halfway the tone grows a reed — formant, breath and all — melodica-close, never a synth trying to be one.
PadsMorph
Waveform, pitch drift and the filter's slow breathing all move together — misty organ, analog drone, today's pad, tape haze, veiled swarm.
SamplerTape
Runs your own recording through old warped tape at one end, a narrow radio speaker at the other.

Every one of these still sounds exactly like today's TowerDub at the knob's resting position — nothing you've already tuned moves on its own.

The desk, live.

Sequencing gets you the riddim. These get you the dub — touched while it's playing, not programmed in advance.

Resonant filter

Dub Filter

A King Tubby–style sweep with a switch per instrument — dial a bus in or out of the filter and ride the cutoff across the whole mix.

Hold to wail

Dub Siren

One knob for Tune, one for Rate, one Mode dial running from a slow wide wail to a fast, alarm-sharp bark. Sends to Delay and Reverb like any other voice.

Feedback held

Delay Freeze

Push feedback toward the edge, then freeze the input — the last echo keeps circling on its own while you play over the top of it.

Live pass-through

Mic Block

A voice, a horn, whatever's in the room — routed straight into the same Delay and Reverb every other instrument shares, off by default.

Live capture · WAV

Dub Record

Hit REC on the transport and TowerDub captures the whole mix live — every filter sweep, siren wail and frozen echo — straight to a WAV file. One take, one export: your own dubplate.
